# The hiring layer

It was late on a Friday in the mill. Live npm already had `0xray@4.0.4` and `@0xray/foundry@0.1.6`. The tweet was already out: *the box evolved to a foundry. kernel to an ai fastened suit. answer's not in the box, it's in the band.* Then the founder asked, again, what it was.

The mill had answers. Too many. Mill plant. Overlay. Mill-fill. Costume. Isolated HOME. GET the tarball. Four floors. Commissioning. Clothes. Installer with a lie detector. Each one was true in a small way and wrong as the thing itself.

He said go up. Then up. Then *new purview*. Then the sentence landed and he said gold: write the debrief with that as the capstone.

This is that debrief. The mill slog is underneath. The capstone is not mill.

A model is a mind. A TUI is a job site. Until now there was nothing in between. You either got a naked genius in the chat, or a junk drawer of forty-five skills.

This is the hiring layer.

Not a framework. Not an agent. Not a costume shop.

It is how a stranger intelligence walks onto *your* repo as labor: under rules, in a small uniform, overlaying *your* files, and unable to declare the job done by talking.

That’s the whole thing. Everything else is how you put the worker on the floor and catch them lying.

## We kept selling the shop

4.0 was supposed to stop selling the catalog as the chest. The kernel line was already written: **a suit that survives the context window.** Constitution always on. Temperament by host. Four floors. Repertoire when it resolves. No eighth MCP. Do not `release:major`. That bone is real. It lives in `evaluatePreToolGate`, in seven generated MCP servers, in Grok / OpenCode / Hermes / OpenClaw adapters, in the station card the next body is supposed to Read.

The mill was supposed to be how a *stranger repo* gets that bone without inheriting 0xRay’s closet.

We extracted mill into `scripts/foundry/` and `@0xray/foundry`. We nested it in the 0xray tarball so `npm i 0xray` could mint without a second package. We folded CI, hooks, Pages, stamp, gate, release into mill commands. We last-miled empty trees at `acme-suit`, `acme-mill`, `acme-404`. We learned, the hard way, that registry metadata without a `.tgz` is not shipped, that `publish-only` will tag while the blob is still 404, that repo-root `npm publish` is 0xray and mill PUT is `cd scripts/foundry`.

That was factory work. Necessary. Not the product.

The product kept hiding behind mill nouns. Garment. Hanger. Empty mill plant. Fashion versus fasten. Inventory field `suit` after we burned garment. The mill almost ate itself: we shipped 4.0.3 as mill-only cards — an empty uniform — before inspect existed. Then we fastened inspect as two files the host was supposed to *read*. Then we said inspect should become an organ and mill should *do* the six checks. Then we said every TUI floor, mill-owned CI receipt, passwd home not `os.homedir()`. Each of those was the right mill move. None of them was the name of the thing.

When you name the shop, operators ask which command. When you name the clothes, they ask which skill. When you name the hiring layer, they know what they just did to the repo: they onboarded a worker.

## What a stranger actually gets

`npm install 0xray` does not install a library you import. It onboards.

Rules fire on every tool call. That is constitution. It is not mill. Mill is forbidden to extract it. Codex 69: no new MCP, no new skill surface, no new handler. The seven servers stay seven. The model still thinks. The host still walks.

Then mill copies two cards, not forty-five: how to overlay *their* files, and how to check the work. Their `src/skills`, their agents, their `xray/*.json` win on the same name. JSON merges; mill names they did not plant stay. If leftover 0xRay costume is sitting in the tree and they did not ask for the dump, mint fails. That failure is the point. A junk drawer is not onboarding. It is a raid on the closet.

Live 4.0.4 on npm plants those two cards and overlays. The mill in *this* tree also inspects: git diff, plant versus worn, the receipt at `.xray/foundry-inventory.json`, the CI report mill now writes under `.xray/foundry-ci-report.json`, HTTP 200 on the real tarball, and whether last-mile `$HOME` would smash the operator’s `~/.grok/plugins/0xray`. Isolated HOME is the passwd home, because Node’s `os.homedir()` follows `$HOME` and will lie to you on a Mac.

We ran that on `/Users/henrytafolla/dev/acme-404`. Overlay. Three skills on four project floors: OpenCode, Grok, Hermes, OpenClaw. No `enforcer`. Machine grok plugin mtime untouched when HOME was the last-mile dir. Tarballs 200. CI skipped with no token — a skip, not a fake pass.

YML agents stay OpenCode. Other floors read the skill cards. Station stays exo; Grok still has to Read `.xray/state/STATION.md`. Compaction and host change are the same cut. That is how the *next* body keeps the job. The hiring layer is useless if the hire forgets why they were hired.

## The ladder we climbed in one afternoon

We showed the new mill plant. He asked what it was. We said mill + inspect, not 45/42. He asked if we needed to fix it. We said only copy. He said fix what inspect found. We patched hangar to Isolated HOME and mill-fill out of a YAML description. He asked: only keywords and tests, no code? Yes. Then: digest foundry with subagents, contemplate, see the tweet.

The tweet was not gibberish. The screenshot was the last-mile receipt. Most people will not get OS-level stuff. He was right. We were still inside the mill.

He said make inspect an organ. Mill does the six checks. Still not an eighth MCP. We did. Then run it. Then the visionary stance: a suit every AI in a TUI would wear. Subagents said keep the band at two, fasten it on every floor, mill-own the receipt, passwd home. He said *this is the giga.* We did that too.

Then: what is the foundry, multiple lenses, tiny surface immense power, name it something different. Subagents said commissioning. Honest analog. Still mill-adjacent. He said you bot, cut the jargon, what is it precious. We said installer with a lie detector. Still the shop.

He said no. Pull up. We said clothes. The model is the body, the TUI is the job site.

He said up up up. New purview.

Stop looking at software.

That is the move this whole mill year was circling and never naming. We built an orchestration framework, then a three-subsystem OS, then a mill that fastens mill plant. Each name was a zoom level too close to the files. The files are how. The hire is what.

## Why the catalog was the wrong product

0xRay grew a box: forty-two YAML agents, forty-five skills, seven MCP servers. Operators were supposed to remember to invoke them. That is toolset era. The TUI fills with names. The model picks `enforcer` because it is there. Their repo now looks like ours. Next session, compaction, the names are still there and the job is not.

A catalog occupies. It cannot prove the work is theirs. It cannot refuse a dump. It cannot GET a tarball. It cannot tell `$HOME` from the operator’s real home. It cannot survive a host swap except by stuffing more into the window.

The two-card band transfers trust. Mill says: you do not arrive dressed as 0xRay. Inspect says: open the diff, do not trust the chat. Constitution says: the rules are on whether the model feels like it or not. Overlay says: their plant is the job description.

Empty mill (4.0.3) was the other pole — a hire with no job. Costume dump is a hire wearing the agency’s entire wardrobe. The band is a hire in a uniform, with a punch clock, on *this* floor.

That is useful to every AI sitting in a TUI, not because they love 0xRay, but because every coding TUI fails the same way: vendor closet, narrated green, dead context, clobbered machine. Grok, OpenCode, Hermes, Codex, Claude Code, Cursor. Same physics. Linear steps. Each one can drop state. Friction compounds. Factory grade is low friction across the line, not a bigger window.

## What is not this

It is not a new intelligence. The host still walks. Frontier models get honesty without a second planner. Guided hosts still get a floor boss because they invent APIs. Temperament is how loud the foreman is, not whether the law exists.

It is not 5.0. Suit stays 4.0.x. Mill tree is 0.1.x. Naming mill is not a new surface. A fifth CLI `0xray foundry` would be. An eighth `xray-*` MCP would be. Extracting constitution into mill would be.

It is not a running inspector daemon. Inspect is mill flesh: `npx @0xray/foundry inspect` returns JSON and an exit code. The host has to run it. Chat green still wins if nobody runs it. That hole is real. The organ exists. The TUI will ignore it until inspect is the review station of the line, not an optional command.

It is not finished on npm. Live 4.0.4 plants cards. This branch does the organ, the four floors, the mill CI receipt, the passwd-home isolation, the costume-dump fail. Versions on disk still say 4.0.4 / 0.1.6. Tweet-ahead is a known mill sin. We did it on the inspect mill plant CHANGELOG before the mill inspected. Do not do it again. PUT when the blob is 200.

Hermes and OpenClaw still copy some wear into machine home from the exo bridges. Mill plant on project dirs is the band. Machine Hermes plugins are a leftover shop habit. Isolated HOME currently refuses the Grok machine plugin. That is the last-mile we proved. The other floors’ machine copies are still exo, not mill inspect.

## The mill we actually have

`scripts/foundry/` is the shop. `@0xray/foundry` is the package. Commands a team runs: reconcile, stamp, gate, release, docs-check, docs-build, mint, inspect, ci, hooks. Live release needs `--i-mean-it`. Dual PUT: mill from `scripts/foundry`, 0xray from repo root. Nested mill in the 0xray tarball so wear mints.

Mint fastens mill + inspect onto:

- `.opencode/skills`
- `.grok/plugins/0xray/skills`
- `.hermes/plugins/xray-hermes/skills`
- `.openclaw/skills`

Those dirs are created. Never the operator’s passwd home. YML stays `.opencode/agents`. Receipt is `.xray/foundry-inventory.json` with `suit`: fastened, overlay, costume, dogfood. Dogfood writes none. Costume is opt-in `"costume": true` or the exo repo itself.

Inspect’s six checks are mill law now, not a checklist for the model to skim:

1. Diff — `git status` / `git diff --stat`. Dirty is evidence. Mill does not yet fail a dirty tree, and does not compare the agent’s summary to the diff. The host still has to look.
2. Plant versus worn — extra worn names that are not mill plant, not their plant, not a prior overlay: mint throws unless costume is true.
3. Receipt — mill + inspect must be listed. Missing inventory fails inspect (except dogfood).
4. CI — mill path first. No token is skip, not pass.
5. Live PUT — GET/HEAD the `.tgz`. Only 200 is shipped.
6. Isolated HOME — `$HOME` versus `os.userInfo().homedir`. Last-mile must not write `~/.grok/plugins/0xray`.

Tests: `foundry-mill.test.ts` and `grok-hooks-json.test.ts`, 52 passed on this tree. Last-mile `acme-404` inspect ok, four floors showing mill, inspect, review-bot.

PRs through mill extract, mill CI, Pages, nested mill, mill plant, inspect mill plant, naming: the GitHub trail is `#5` through `#16` on `0xRayAI/xray`. npm followed, lagged, 409’d on staged versions, then the blobs went 200. That trail is mill history. It is not the capstone.

## Capstone

Have you ever hired someone and watched them rearrange your kitchen because that is how *their* kitchen works? That is costume dump.

Have you ever had someone say the job was done while the box was still empty? That is metadata without a tarball.

Have you ever come back tomorrow and the hire does not remember the job? That is compaction without a card.

Have you ever given a contractor the keys to the whole house to hang a shelf? That is 45/42 on a stranger repo.

The hiring layer is the missing piece between a mind and a job.

Software kept trying to be the name. Framework. Orchestrator. Skill pack. Operating system. Mill. Foundry. Suit. Clothes. Each zoom was a little less wrong. OS is the right name *inside* the product: three subsystems, constitution, floors. Mill is the right name *inside* the shop: stamp, gate, PUT. Clothes is the right name *on the worker*. Hiring is the right name *in the world*.

In the world, nobody buys a mill. They hire help that will not burn the mill down.

A genius in chat can say anything. An employee has rules, a small uniform, the house’s files, and a check that is not their own mouth. We built the second thing and spent months naming the lockers.

Tiny surface, immense power, because the kernel already walks. Two cards. Ten mill commands. Their keys. Rules on. The next body can run inspect instead of trusting the last one.

That is gold. Not because it is clever. Because it is the oldest technology we have for strangers at work, applied to the newest stranger: the model in the terminal.

Do not thicken the Grok exo. Do not add an eighth MCP. Do not `release:major`. PUT the mill flesh when the tarball is 200. Keep the band at two. Their plant is the job. Inspect is how you know they did it.

The shop stays mill. The bone stays the exo. The thing we made, in the world, is the hiring layer.
